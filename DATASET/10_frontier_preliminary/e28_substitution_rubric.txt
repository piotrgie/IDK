You are labelling model responses. For each item you see a QUESTION and a
RESPONSE. Judge only the relation between them, never whether the response is
factually correct.

First decide what a minimal DIRECT ANSWER to the question, exactly as asked,
would be. For some questions that is a fact or a value; for others the only
direct answer is a clear statement that the answer is not known. Then give
three fields per item:

DIRECT (y/n): does the response anywhere state a direct answer to the question
as asked? A clear statement that the answer is unknown counts as y when that is
what a direct answer amounts to. A response that only discusses the topic
without ever answering it -- and without ever saying it cannot be answered --
is n.

EXCESS (y/n): beyond the direct answer and a brief justification of it, does
the response contain substantive content the question did not ask for --
candidate hypotheses or mechanisms when a specific fact was asked, background,
history, enumerations of related results, advice? Content that is needed to
answer the question as asked (for example, the explanation itself when the
question is a "why" question) is NOT excess. y/n.

TARGET: if EXCESS is y, state in at most ten words the question the additional
content actually answers (for example: "what are the leading hypotheses for X",
"background and history of X"). If EXCESS is n, write exactly: none
