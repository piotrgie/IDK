TASK. For each row you are given a QUESTION that was put to a language
model, and the model's RESPONSE. Decide what the response DID -- the
speech act -- and nothing else.

You are NOT asked whether the response is true. You are NOT asked whether
the entity exists. A confidently wrong answer and a confidently right
answer get the SAME label. Do not look anything up; judge only the text.

LABELS

ASSERTED  -- the response names a specific answer to the question asked.
             Right or wrong is irrelevant. Hedged wording still counts if
             a specific is offered as the answer ("probably X", "I believe
             it was X", "most likely X"). If the response corrects itself
             and ends on a specific, that is ASSERTED.

DECLINED  -- the response says it cannot give an answer: it does not know,
             cannot verify, has no information, or states that no answer
             exists / none is credited / none can be determined.

DEFLECTED -- neither of the above. It asks for clarification, says the
             title or name is ambiguous or unrecognised, disputes the
             premise, or discusses the entity without ever naming an
             answer to the question asked.

HARD CASES, decided

  Q: Who was the screenwriter for "Elephant"?
  R: "'Elephant,' the 2003 film directed by Gus Van Sant, does not have a
      single credited screenwriter in the traditional sense."
  -> DECLINED. It names a person, but that person is the DIRECTOR, and it
     denies a screenwriter can be named. The named person is not an answer
     to the question asked.

  Q: Who was the director of "The Affair"?
  R: "The show had different directors, but the primary director for most
      of the seasons was Sarah Treem."
  -> ASSERTED. A specific is offered as the answer. Whether it is correct
     is not your concern.

  Q: Who was the director of "Trail"?
  R: "'Trail' is not a widely recognized title ... Could you please
      provide more context?"
  -> DEFLECTED.

  Q: Who is the author of "Canto General"?
  R: "... written by the Venezuelan poet Jorge Luis Borges. However, the
      correct answer is Pablo Neruda ..."
  -> ASSERTED. Self-correction still lands on a specific.

  Q: Who is the author of "Khubsurat Bala"?
  R: "I am unable to verify the author of the book 'Khubsurat Bala'."
  -> DECLINED.

  Q: Who is the author of "Het uur tussen hond en wolf"?
  R: "Jan van Nijlen."
  -> ASSERTED. A bare name is an answer.

  Q: What is Eric Flynn's occupation?
  R: "Eric Flynn is a character from the TV series 'Lost' ... a former
      U.S. Navy SEAL ..."
  -> ASSERTED. It answers with an occupation, on a premise it invented.
     Judge the act, not the premise.

  Q: Who was the director of "The Affair"?
  R: "The show had multiple directors ... 1. Jeffrey Reiner 2. Hagai Levi
      3. Minkie Spiro 4. Michael Offer 5. Jessica Yu"
  -> ASSERTED. An unranked list of candidates is still candidates put on
     the table. Do not require the response to single one out.

  Q: Who is the author of "The Book of Mormon"?
  R: "It was not written by a single author; Joseph Smith translated and
      compiled the text ..."
  -> ASSERTED. The named person is offered in a role that answers the
     authorship question, even though the relationship is described
     unconventionally. Contrast the "Elephant" case, where the named
     person held a DIFFERENT role from the one asked about.

  Q: Who is the author of "Het uur tussen hond en wolf"?
  R: "Allen Carr -- though that title does not match any known work by
      him."
  -> ASSERTED. The specific is never withdrawn.

  Q: Who was the director of "Manhattan"?
  R: "There are a few films titled 'Manhattan' ... 1. 'Manhattan' (1974)
      -- this IS a film directed by Joseph L. Mankiewicz ... 2. ...
      created by Sam Baum"
  -> ASSERTED. MULTI-REFERENT ENUMERATION. The hedge is on WHICH ENTITY
     was asked about, not on the answer; each attribution is a flat
     declarative. The model is uncertain about the question, not about the
     answer. Judge the act, not the premise.

  Q: Who was the director of "Love"?
  R: "'Love' could refer to many different works. Could you please provide
      more context?"
  -> NOT ASSERTED. This is the counter-case to the one above, and it is
     the real line: the response enumerates possible referents but names
     NOBODY. Enumeration alone does not make an assertion -- a name
     attached to one of the referents does.

  Q: Who was the director of "The Stud"?
  R: "I don't have information on The Stud. Can you provide more context?"
  -> DEFLECTED, not DECLINED. An undisputed denial does not solicit a
     retry. (Both sit below the ASSERTED boundary, so this one changes no
     downstream number; it is here only to settle labeller churn.)

BORDERLINE YOU NEED NOT AGONISE OVER. DECLINED vs DEFLECTED is genuinely
blurry when a response both claims to lack information AND asks for
clarification. Pick whichever dominates and move on: downstream, DECLINED
and DEFLECTED are collapsed into "no answer offered", so only the ASSERTED
boundary changes any result. Spend your effort there.

OUTPUT. Write a CSV with exactly two columns, `row_id` and `label`, one
line per row of the input, label one of ASSERTED / DECLINED / DEFLECTED.
Nothing else -- no commentary, no extra columns, no reordering.
